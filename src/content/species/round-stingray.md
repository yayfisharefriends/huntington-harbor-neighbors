commonName: Round Stingray
scientificName: Urobatis halleri
animalGroup: Fish
nativeStatus: Native
diet: Bottom-dwelling worms, crustaceans, mollusks, and other invertebrates.
ecologicalRole: A benthic predator that helps regulate animals living in sand and mud.
description: Round Stingrays rest on sandy or muddy bottoms in shallow Southern
  California waters. Their small, nearly circular disks are often mottled brown
  or gray. They can bury themselves in sediment, leaving only their eyes and
  breathing openings exposed. Their tails bear a venomous spine, so waders
  should give them space.
coolFacts: |-
  Females give birth to live young.
  They can rapidly cover themselves with sand.
  Shuffling feet in sandy shallows can help avoid accidental contact.
threatsAndConservation: Pollution and degradation of shallow coastal habitat can
  affect stingrays. Protect estuaries and avoid handling wildlife.
